Buying Peanut Butter in India: A Practical Comparison Checklist

Compare peanut butter in India by ingredients, texture, serving size and price per 100g, with a practical checklist for buying online.

By the NJOY Journal12 October 20264 min read

Start with the job your jar needs to do

Searching for the best peanut butter in India quickly produces a wall of ratings, discounts and protein claims. A better starting point is your own kitchen. Do you want a plain spread for toast, a chocolate treat, a sauce ingredient or something that blends easily into oats? Write that down before comparing brands. A product that suits one use can be inconvenient for another, even when both are well made.

Also consider how often you will open the jar. A large pack may look economical, but it only saves money if you enjoy it and use it within its labelled dates. For a first purchase, a smaller jar can be a sensible way to learn which flavour and texture you actually prefer.

Compare exact products rather than brand names

Pintola, MYFITNESS and NJOY sell into the wider nut butter category, but a brand name alone does not describe every jar. Plain, sweetened, chocolate and other flavoured variants need separate comparisons. MYFITNESS, for example, lists smooth and crunchy products on its official website. Its product descriptions and packaging should be checked for the specific variant you are considering.

Use official product pages as a starting point and the delivered package as your final reference. Product ranges and recipes can change. If the website description, photograph and nutrition panel disagree, ask the seller to confirm the current label before ordering. Avoid treating an old review or a marketplace image as a permanent specification.

Make the numbers comparable

Compare like with like: the same unit of weight, the same nutritional basis and the same delivery conditions. A price for one jar says little when the other jar is a different size. Price per 100g is purchase price divided by net weight in grams, multiplied by 100. Check whether delivery charges change the total. Keep grams and millilitres separate; volume is not automatically the same as food weight.

For nutrition, compare the per-100g panels first, then calculate what your usual portion provides. A fictional label showing 24g protein per 100g would provide 3.6g in a weighed 15g portion. This is a calculation example, not a nutrition claim for any NJOY product. The same method works for energy, sugar and sodium.

Check the ingredients that matter to you

Decide whether you want peanuts alone, peanuts with salt, or a sweeter spread. Check added sugar, oils and flavour ingredients rather than assuming every peanut butter uses the same recipe. Organic and other certification claims should be supported on the actual pack. A short list may match your preferences, but list length alone is not a complete measure of food quality.

Check the allergen declaration as well. Peanuts and tree nuts require careful attention when buying for someone else. FSSAI's labelling guidance is useful for understanding declarations on Indian packaged foods. If an allergy is involved, obtain a clear answer about the exact product and shared processing before choosing it.

A useful shortlist for your next order

Choose two or three suitable jars and record the exact variant, net quantity, delivered price, texture and storage instructions. Then choose based on the meal you intend to make. This small comparison is more useful than collecting dozens of promotional claims.

For custom quantities or mixed gifts, add your selection to the cart and request the current quote. There is no need to guess the final cost of a custom order.

Your questions, answered

How do I compare peanut butter prices fairly?

Compare delivered price per 100g when both products declare weight in grams. Do not directly equate a volume in millilitres with a weight in grams.

Is the peanut butter with the biggest protein number always the best choice?

Compare ingredients and protein on the same serving basis, then choose for your intended use. The largest headline number alone does not establish the best product for you.
