Diwali Gift Hampers for Teams: A Corporate Ordering Checklist

Plan Diwali corporate gift hampers with a clear brief for quantities, nut butter selection, greeting cards, delivery and quote approval.

By the NJOY Journal12 October 20264 min read

Write a brief before choosing the box

A successful corporate Diwali order starts with clear logistics. Before selecting a hamper, write down who will receive it, how many you need and where it will go. Employee gifts, client gifts and a shared office box may need different formats. The right starting point is the recipient's experience, then the packaging around it.

Decide who can approve the selection and quote. One person should consolidate feedback so the supplier does not receive conflicting versions of the same order. If several teams are involved, collect their quantities in one document before asking for the final proposal. This makes changes easier to track.

Choose a selection people can use

For a mixed group, familiar flavours can make a useful starting point, with one distinctive jar to give the gift personality. A peanut or almond butter alongside a chocolate or pistachio choice offers different serving possibilities. This is a planning suggestion, not a guarantee that every recipient will like the same combination.

Ask about ingredient preferences and allergy requirements before finalising the contents. Peanuts and tree nuts are relevant allergens; a seed-based product is not automatically suitable for someone with a nut allergy because shared processing must also be considered. Where a recipient has a restriction, confirm the exact product information instead of making a substitution by name alone.

Make the quote specific

A useful quotation should identify the chosen products, quantities, box type, any greeting-card requirements, delivery arrangement and applicable charges. If you have a budget range, share it alongside the brief. This gives the owner a concrete selection to price rather than a vague request for a premium hamper.

For NJOY's Diwali offers, add the preferred items to the cart and request a quote. If your cart also includes products with published prices, those items can remain part of the selection while the custom portion is priced separately. Review the complete quote before treating the order total as confirmed.

Agree the delivery plan early

Choose between delivery to one office and separate recipient addresses. A single office delivery may need internal distribution, while individual deliveries require accurate contact and address details. Collect only the information needed for fulfilment and share it through the agreed order channel. Confirm which team owns corrections before dispatch.

Ask the supplier to confirm preparation timing, dispatch timing and the scope of delivery rather than assuming a promised arrival date from a general page. Build in time for approvals and address checking. For food gifts, also ask about the labelled dates so the recipient has time to enjoy the contents.

Keep the greeting useful and personal

A short, carefully proofread card can do more for the gift than a long promotional message. Use the correct organisation name, a clear seasonal greeting and an appropriate sign-off. Ask for confirmation of any custom design or card before the order is finalised. Do not assume optional branding is included unless it appears in the quote.

A small serving idea can help recipients enjoy an unfamiliar nut butter: try it on toast, with fruit or in a separately mixed dressing. Direct them to the product label for ingredients and storage. If you include a link to NJOY's Instagram, present it as inspiration rather than a substitute for the pack details.

Approve one final order sheet

Before paying or confirming dispatch, check the complete selection against the approved quote. Count the boxes, read the card text and check the destination list. Make one final version that both sides recognise. This reduces last-minute ambiguity when a festive order contains several moving parts.

After delivery, record practical feedback such as packaging condition, address issues and which selection worked well. That gives you a useful brief for the next occasion. Corporate gifting becomes simpler when each order improves the process instead of starting from a blank page every year.

  • Recipient groups and confirmed quantities.
  • Exact products, packaging and card text.
  • Written quote with agreed delivery arrangements.
  • One final approval owner and order version.

Your questions, answered

How do I get a price for an NJOY Diwali hamper?

Add your preferred Diwali selection to the cart and request a quote with quantity and packaging needs. Treat the final price as confirmed only when the owner shares and you approve the quote.

Can I assume all recipients can eat the same nut butter hamper?

No. Ask about relevant restrictions and confirm the exact ingredients and shared-processing information. Arrange an appropriate alternative where needed.
